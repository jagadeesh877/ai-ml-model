The Fox and the Grapes.
A hungry fox saw some fine bunches of ripe grapes hanging from a vine trained along a high trellis.
He did his best to reach them by jumping as high into the air as he could, but all in vain, for they were just out of reach.
After many leaps, he sat down panting and looked at the fruit in disappointment.
Turning away with his nose in the air, he said, "I am sure they are sour anyway, and not fit for a gentleman to eat."
It is easy to despise what you cannot get.

The Tortoise and the Hare.
A hare was once making fun of a tortoise for being so slow upon his feet.
"Do you ever get anywhere?" laughed the hare with a boastful grin.
"Yes," replied the tortoise calmly, "and I get there sooner than you think. Run with me and I will prove it."
The hare accepted the challenge with laughter, thinking it was a fine joke.
The fox was chosen to be the judge, and he marked the distance and started the runners.
The hare dashed off swiftly and was soon far out of sight.
Knowing that he had plenty of time, the hare laid down beside the dusty road to take a pleasant nap in the warm sun.
Meanwhile, the tortoise kept walking step by step, never pausing or looking back.
He kept on toward the finish line with quiet determination.
When the hare finally woke up, the sun was sinking behind the hills.
He leaped up and ran as fast as his legs could carry him, but it was too late.
He arrived at the goal only to find the tortoise resting peacefully, having won the race.
Slow and steady wins the race.

The Lion and the Mouse.
A great lion was sleeping peacefully in the shade of a wide tree.
A little mouse, not knowing where she was going, ran across the lion's mighty nose.
Waking with a roar, the lion placed his huge paw upon the tiny creature to crush her.
"Please, King of the Forest," cried the little mouse, "spare my life! If you let me go, I may be able to help you someday."
The lion laughed loudly at the thought of a tiny mouse ever helping a mighty king, but being generous, he lifted his paw and let her go.
Some time later, while hunting in the dark forest, the lion was caught in a hunter's strong net.
Unable to break the thick ropes, the lion roared with anger and sorrow until the forest shook.
The little mouse recognized the deep voice and ran swiftly to the spot.
Using her sharp teeth, she gnawed through the heavy ropes until the lion was free.
"You laughed at me once," said the mouse softly, "thinking that a mouse could never aid a lion. Now you see that kindness is never wasted."
Even the smallest friend may prove of great help.

The Wind and the Sun.
A dispute once arose between the North Wind and the Sun as to which was the stronger.
While they were debating with great energy, a traveler came walking along the road wrapped in a warm cloak.
"Let us agree," said the Sun, "that whichever of us can cause that traveler to take off his cloak shall be acknowledged the victor."
"Agreed," roared the North Wind.
The North Wind began to blow with all his might.
A cold blast swept across the valley, howling through the tall trees and scattering brown leaves.
At the first gust, the traveler pulled his cloak tightly around his shoulders.
The harder the cold wind blew, the tighter the traveler held his heavy garment.
At last, exhausted and breathless, the North Wind gave up the attempt.
Then the warm Sun came out from behind the gray clouds.
He shone down with gentle radiance upon the traveler's head.
The pleasant warmth soon drove away the bitter chill.
The traveler unbuttoned his cloak and walked along happily.
As the heat increased, he grew weary and sat down beside a clear brook, casting his heavy cloak aside.
Persuasion is more powerful than force.
Gentleness often accomplishes what harshness cannot.

The Ant and the Grasshopper.
On a bright winter day, a colony of ants was busy drying grain that they had gathered during the summer.
A starving grasshopper, with his fiddle tucked under his shivering arm, came creeping up and begged for a little food.
"What did you do all summer?" asked one of the ants in surprise. "Did you not store any grain for the winter months?"
"No," sighed the grasshopper, "I had no time to store food. The weather was so lovely that I spent my days singing and playing music."
The ant turned back to his work with a firm shake of the head.
"If you were foolish enough to sing all summer," said the ant, "you must dance hungry to bed in the winter."
There is a time for work and a time for play.
Careful preparation brings peace and safety.
